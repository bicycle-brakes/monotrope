**The valve mechanism that exists — for rules, not for gates.** `scripts/_verdict.py` defines the vocabulary: `Level` (INFO/DRIFT/BLOCK) and `Ramp` (name, condition, predicate, target BLOCK, until_then, optional deadline), where `Ramp.due` fires on a passed deadline or a true predicate, and `Verdict.render` prints "[valve …] reported as … instead of …, until: …". Its docstring states the doctrine: a valve without a due check is "an exception under a better name", and the deadline exists because a predicate measures the stock, never the work. `scripts/ramp_valves.py` applies it to three kinds of object: three ruff rule groups (`WARTENDE_LINT_GRUPPEN`: E5, PL, RUF — none is in `ruff.toml`'s `select` today, so all three valves exist), the provenance second-handle valve, and twenty rule valves (`REGEL_VENTILE`, each naming an enforcer test and a deadline 2026-11-30 or 2026-12-31; two closed valves are documented in comments: merge-freshness closed 2026-10-08, ruleset-assert closed 2026-10-05). The gate `ramp-valves` (verify, block, seconds) runs that script, so a due valve turns every local and hosted run red. `tests/test_regelwerk.py` couples rules and valves in both directions: rules below level 3 must name a dated `Ramp:` valve present in `ramp_valves.valves()`, and `test_every_named_ramp_exists_and_no_valve_is_orphaned` forbids orphaned valves — a valve that belongs to no rule is red unless its name starts `lint-` or is `provenance-review`, the two declared exceptions. No gate of `decisions/gates.yaml` has a valve anywhere in this mechanism today.
