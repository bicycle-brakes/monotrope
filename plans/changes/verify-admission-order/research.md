## What exists today

**The admission engine and its order.** `scripts/verify_lock.py` is one machine-wide admission for protected verification work of one user, shared across repositories and worktrees through the state root `${XDG_STATE_HOME:-~/.local/state}/coding-harness/` (module docstring): the work lock `verify.lock` (`fcntl.flock`), the published ownership `verify.holder.json` (pid, token, command, start, admission id, measured wait), one queue record per waiter in `verify.queue/`, and the short-held `verify.registry.lock` that serialises registration, pruning and turn-taking. The docstring states the rule directly: "registration order is the admission order — `flock` alone supplies no FIFO, so only the first live record may try the work lock." It is implemented in `_register` (claims the highest existing 20-digit registration number plus one, named `<number>-<pid>.json`, held open under a liveness flock), `_live_records_locked` (sorted listing in registration order), `_queue_wait` (only `records[0]` may `_try_acquire_work`; then `_leave_queue_locked`), and `_prune_locked` (a dead waiter's record is removed under the registry lock). This order came from the archived change `plans/changes/archive/2026-11-06-verify-machine-lock/spec.md` (bead coding-harness-p6mu, criterion 2: "several live entries wait, they are admitted in registration order without runner priority"; criterion 3: the wait is logged immediately and at least every five minutes, naming holder pid, command and start). That spec's non-goals explicitly exclude "Priority scheduling, a public bypass flag, or CI-specific disabling."

**Wait visibility today, and the asymmetry with the memory gate.** `_queue_wait` (`scripts/verify_lock.py`) logs `waiting for admission: <holder>` immediately, repeats `still waiting for admission (Ns)` every `QUEUE_LOG_SECONDS = 300.0`, and logs `admitted after Ns` at entry; the loop is `while True` with **no deadline and no alarm** — the only bound that refuses is on the memory side, where `_memory_gate` waits below the floor (`DEFAULT_MEMORY_FLOOR` 25 %, `HARNESS_VERIFY_MEMORY_FLOOR`, recheck every `MEMORY_POLL_SECONDS = 30.0`) and raises `MemoryFloorRefused` after `MEMORY_DEADLINE_SECONDS = 3600.0` below the floor. No alarm mechanism exists anywhere in `scripts/` (a Grep for `alarm` matches only the `.beads/hooks/*` perl `alarm` wrappers and unrelated uses), and `docs/WORKFLOW.md`, "Night runs", records for the night profile: "until the maintainer chooses a push service — no phone alert and no independent heartbeat exists." The pins are `tests/test_verify_lock.py` Criterion 2 (`test_live_waiters_enter_in_registration_order`, whose comment notes the test verifies the queue's numbering against the real registration order, review row L3-2) and Criterion 3 (`test_queue_wait_logs_live_holder_and_progress`).

**Who queues, and who does not.** All entry kinds share the one queue: the shell path (verify_all.sh execs the scheduler), the runner's scheduler call, and direct `hold_admission` callers (pinned together by `tests/test_verify_lock.py` Criterion 1, `test_independent_entries_share_one_machine_lock`, with a `DIRECT_BODY` entry that mirrors how `scripts.stage2` holds admission). A nested protected entry — a descendant carrying `HARNESS_VERIFY_LOCK_HELD`/`HARNESS_VERIFY_LOCK_TOKEN` validated against the published ownership (`nested_holder_pid`) — bypasses the queue entirely and inherits its ancestor's admission ("inheriting admission from pid …; no queue and no memory wait", `hold_admission`). Each admission publishes a non-secret context — `admission_id` (uuid hex) and the measured queue-plus-memory `wait_seconds` — into the holder record and the protected child's environment (`admission_context()`, `ADMISSION_ID_ENV`/`ADMISSION_WAIT_ENV`; bead coding-harness-1um6), with inherited waits marked `reused` and never re-counted.

**Where admission sits in the run.** `scripts/verify_run.py::run_verification` runs every unprotected gate (declared `seconds`, unmarked) first, then takes exactly one admission via `verify_lock.hold_admission` around the protected set (the `minutes` gates and `seconds` gates marked `needs_admission: true`; partition at `cheap = [g for g in selection if not g["admission"]]` / `protected = …`), defaulting the holder record's command to `["verify-run", job]` (`admission_command`). The measured wait is returned on `RunResult.admission_wait_seconds` and, for the runner, extends the budget deadline so waits consume no execution allowance; `scripts/stage2.py::_scheduled_verify` calls the same scheduler in-process ("this caller passes only the launch callback, never a whole-run admission of its own") with the shared `VERIFY_SECONDS` allowance. `scripts/verify_all.sh` is the interpreter bootstrap that execs this scheduler (header comment, bead coding-harness-bxxt.6). Which gates are protected comes from `decisions/gates.yaml` (schema 3; `cost` seconds/minutes required; optional `needs_admission` boolean — header: "No gate needs it today", per the audit of 2026-11-14 in `plans/changes/verify-cheap-before-admission/seconds-gate-audit.md`); the `minutes` gates today are `pytest` and `leerprobe`.

**Where waits are recorded, and where they are not read.** `scripts/verify_duration.py` stores `admission_wait_seconds`, `admission_id`, `admission_reused` and `admission_requested` on the run's records and prints them in the summary line ("execution X · admission wait Y") and in `report`/`report --run <id>`. No report aggregates waits: `scripts/run_report.py` (the `make report` tool) has no admission or wait reading (Grep: no matches), and `scripts/machine_facts.py` explicitly refuses nothing ("not a gate: no threshold is compared and nothing is refused"). The night run's only wait supervision is the 30-minute wake routine (docs/WORKFLOW.md, "The 30-minute wake", bead coding-harness-7x3c): the orchestrating session checks each lane's activity at each wake and appends one summary line to `lane-watch.log`; "The wake is time-based: it returns even when every lane looks busy, and lane idleness alone triggers nothing."

**Demand evidence and the bead's origin.** `docs/research/2026-11-09-make-verify-long-term.md` measured the slot as the bottleneck (one ~22-minute slot, ~65 runs/day capacity, lanes asking 34–43, waits median 22–30 minutes, maximum 90) and lists "admission order and wait alarm (ofmz)" under the night-run epic; its closing order-of-work note says the bead pair "stays useful for machine load, but lose[s] urgency for the full check once steps 1 and 2 hold." The bead originated on 2026-11-02 as "admission order and a stuck-wait alarm" (`docs/journal/handover/2026-11-02-five-lane-night.md`, "Seven orchestration gaps became beads … discovered from coding-harness-4emv"). The latest measured wait figure is prose in `docs/journal/handover/2026-11-20-day-run.md`: "One verify slot remained the bottleneck: repairs waited 15–55 minutes for admission (beads coding-harness-u1ha, coding-harness-ofmz)." There is one slot: `verify.lock` is a single exclusive file and no second-slot code exists on this branch; the two-slots bead (coding-harness-u1ha) has no change folder in this tree (Glob) — the 2026-11-20 handover lists it as a lane on branch `feat/verify-two-slots`, not merged here.

**A naming overlap to keep apart.** "Admission" also names the model-call admission in `scripts/stage2.py` (paid terms readings, free-model admission, `admission_problem`, `go_admission`, `free_admission_problem`) — unrelated to the machine verify admission; the two live in different files and must not be conflated.

## What is unclear about it

- The bead's exact remaining scope and state: the tracker was not read in this phase (no rendered copy is committed, and the reader `scripts/bead_render.py` was not run). What ofmz's "admission order" should add beyond the implemented registration-order FIFO (delivered by p6mu), and what form the "stuck-wait alarm" takes, is defined in no repository artifact read here. This is the interview's first question.
- "Wait alarm" could mean a louder log line, a threshold warning, a refusal (as the memory side already has after 60 minutes), a night-run wake note, or a push notification; no artifact states which. The night profile explicitly records that no push service exists (`docs/WORKFLOW.md`, "Night runs"), and the night-runs house rule keeps runs plain (no service or dashboard).
- Whether an alarm binds the queue wait only or also the memory wait: today the queue wait has no bound while the memory wait refuses after 60 minutes — an asymmetry, not a decision anywhere.
- Whether waits should surface beyond stderr and the duration history: `run_report.py` and `machine_facts.py` read none today; the 15–55-minute figure exists only as handover prose, not as a report.
- Whether any ordering policy beyond registration FIFO is wanted: p6mu's spec names priority scheduling a non-goal, so any order change crosses that boundary and would have to say so.
- Whether nested (inherited) entries belong in wait accounting: they bypass the queue by design and mark their wait reused.
- The interplay with the two-slots change (bead coding-harness-u1ha): its artefacts are not in this tree and its tracker state was not read; a second slot changes the queue/lock shape an admission-order change would build on.
- The live figures behind "the slot is the bottleneck" come from handovers and the 2026-11-09 research note; no current aggregated wait report exists to re-measure against.
