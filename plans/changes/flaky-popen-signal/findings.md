| S-7 | MAJOR | SPEC | phase 2a | spec.md AC2 (l.33–34), AC7 (l.43–44), Open decisions (l.75) | [CANNOT GO RED] On the worker thread, AC2 does not require the cancellation to be what ends the child. It only requires the child to be gone "before that launch returns or raises". A worker's `launch` never gets the signal's exception, because Python runs signal handlers on the main thread. The worker learns of cancellation only through the `_LAUNCH_STOPPED` recheck (`scripts/stage2.py:1083`) or through `kill_live`'s `killpg`. Every other way a timed launch ends already reaps its leader: normal `communicate`, the timeout kill path and the `NotStarted` path (`scripts/stage2.py:1085–1097`). Case that should refute AC2 but does not: worker launches ignore cancellation entirely, with no recheck and no `kill_live` kill. With the test's `/bin/sleep 30` and `timeout=60`, the joined worker's launch returns once the child exits on its own, with the leader reaped, so AC2 stays green. The CI failure behind the bead (main returned, child still alive 5 s later) does not refute AC2 either, as long as the worker reaps later. Only AC7's "natural exit" clause limits this, and that clause applies to the test verdict, not to the launch contract, and does not cover a launch that ends at its own timeout. Two readings, both keeping S-3's per-launch choice: (A) the cancellation itself must end the launch, through `NotStarted` or the cancellation's kill, before the child's natural exit and before the launch's own timeout. (B) Any end of the launch counts. Under A, how soon the main-thread handler reaches a launch already in progress is part of the contract. That handler can be delayed while main is blocked, for example in `join`. Under B it is not part of the contract, and the worker case is judged only through AC7. The spec also does not say how long the test may wait for the worker it must join before the verdict, while AC7 forbids "a longer watchdog". | Opus | open | — |
